Everything Glass does.

Most remote desktops ride the same pipeline as video calls, which buffers to look smooth. Glass hands frames from the hardware encoder straight to your browser's decoder and never waits.

Feels local
Hardware H.264 over WebTransport, decoded off the main thread. A live meter shows the real latency.
Just a browser
Open a link on your iPad. Add it to the Home Screen for a fullscreen app with your shortcuts intact.
Every display
Live previews of every screen along the bottom: tap one to switch, or see them all side by side, arranged like your desk.
Every machine
All your computers in one menu. Switch between them without leaving the page.
Full control
Trackpad, keyboard, touch and pinch to zoom. Apple Pencil draws with pressure and tilt. Unlock a locked Mac. ⌘Tab works.
Sound, clipboard, files
System audio, copy and paste both ways, and files sent directly between your devices.
Your files, browsable
Browse the computer's folders with previews, download or drag a file out, upload straight into any folder, rename and move to the Trash.
Steady on bad Wi-Fi
Adapts its bitrate to the network, reconnects by itself, and never shows a corrupted frame.
From anywhere
Away from home, glass link --anywhere connects any browser straight to your computer: end-to-end encrypted, no ports opened.
Links that work once
Let a phone or a friend's laptop in with a one-time link. Each device gets its own pass; revoke them all with one command.
Links with limits
View-only, no files, no clipboard, an hour and done. Ask first, or require Face ID or Touch ID every time.
Sharp text
For code and documents: native size, 4:2:2 colour while things move, and a lossless 4:4:4 picture once the screen is still.
Helping someone
Chat, draw on their screen, blank it and block its keyboard and mouse while you work, and see who connected when.
Beyond the desktop
One app's window on its own, a terminal you can resize or make full screen, local web ports from your tailnet, and session recording.
Your keyboard, per machine
⌘ and Ctrl swaps remembered for each computer, and a shortcut bar for touch screens.
Controllers and mic
Your microphone reaches the computer, so its apps hear you (a Mac needs the free BlackHole driver). Game controllers reach a Linux computer as if plugged in.
Wake and restart
Wake a sleeping machine from the menu, or restart Glass or the whole computer and reconnect.
A second display
Make your iPad, or any screen with a browser, one more display beside your Mac's. Like Sidecar, with nothing to install.
A real Mac app
Setup in a few clicks, then everything the command line does in a native, GPU-drawn window: who's connected, links with QR codes, machines, history and settings.
AI agents, supervised
Add Glass to Claude or Cursor in one click. You see every step, and pause, stop or approve it before it sends, buys or deletes.
Notifications
On your phone, iPad or laptop, even with Glass closed: an agent needs you, someone asks to connect (allow it from the notification), or a message. End-to-end encrypted.
An app on every device
Install Glass from the browser: a badge, a shortcut to each of your machines, share photos and files to the Mac, picture in picture, and it opens even when the computer is asleep.
A console
Who's connected, requests to allow, links with QR codes, history, access rules and an address book of your machines.
For teams
Sign in with Google, Entra, Okta or LDAP. Access rules per group, SCIM provisioning and device posture checks.
Self-hostable
Run the remote-access relay yourself and keep glassdesk.dev out of the path entirely.
Private by default
On your own Tailscale network, your devices get in and nothing else does. Remote access stays off until you turn it on.

Two minutes to set up.

  1. InstallPaste the one-liner on the computer you want to control. It checks the download before installing.
  2. Follow alongThe setup connects Tailscale and walks you through the permissions, checking each one as you go.
  3. Scan and goScan the QR code with your iPad. Glass starts with the computer from now on, and keeps itself up to date.

Or let your agent do it.

Glass comes with a skill for AI agents. Ask Claude Code to set up Glass on my Mac mini and it finds the machine on your tailnet, installs Glass over SSH, and tells you exactly which switch to click on that screen. Later, just ask it for a link to your computer.

$ mkdir -p ~/.claude/skills/glass && curl -fsSL https://glassdesk.dev/skill.md -o ~/.claude/skills/glass/SKILL.md

Tell us what you think.

Bugs, ideas, what you'd use it for. It goes straight to the people building Glass. You can also run glass feedback "…" or open a GitHub issue.
